Part 1 · The Problem

The Moment
of Truth

The most important email of the quarter arrives while you're gone. Customers don't schedule their urgency around your calendar — and they don't wait for Monday.

A facility goes dark
Outages, failed inspections, safety findings — urgent by nature, and exactly our business.
A budget gets approved
Capital frees up on a random Tuesday. The money moves whether you're in or not.
A competitor stumbles
Their miss is your opening — but only if you're the one who answers.
Part 1 · The Problem

What Your Auto-Reply Actually Says

You wrote
“I'm out of the office until Monday.”
They hear: “Your problem can wait. Call someone who's in.”
You wrote
“I'll have limited access to email.”
They hear: “I might see this. I might not. Good luck.”
You wrote
“For urgent matters, contact…”
They hear: “Start over with a stranger who doesn't know your account.”
The truth
It's the only message we send that says we're unavailable
No other part of the FSG brand tells a customer “not now.” Why would your inbox?
Part 2 · Why It Matters

Speed Is the
Tiebreaker

In facilities services, the trigger events are urgent by nature. Whoever responds first usually writes the scope everyone else has to bid against.

First in frames the problem
The first rep in the conversation shapes the scope, the spec, and the budget.
Silence creates shoppers
Every hour without a reply is an hour the customer spends finding your competitor.
Responsiveness is the brand
Customers forgive a lot. They remember who picked up when it mattered.
Part 3 · The Objection

“So I Never
Get to Unplug?”

Wrong takeaway. Take the vacation. Take the whole week. This isn't about being always on — it's about your pipeline never being off. The auto-reply broadcasts your absence. Coverage makes it invisible.

Coverage, not silence
Part 3 · The Fix

The Coverage Playbook

Step 1
Warn your hot deals personally
Before you leave, a short personal note to active opportunities: who's covering, and their direct line.
Step 2
Name a wingman
Brief a teammate on your open deals in Engage. They answer as a colleague who knows the account — not a stranger.
Step 3
Route, don't announce
Forward or delegate the inbox and phone. The customer gets an answer — never a notice that you're gone.
Step 4
One 10-minute sweep
If you choose to check in, one short daily scan for deal-critical items only. Everything else waits — covered.
Part 4 · The Standard

Out of Office vs.
Always in Business

The irony: the rep with the auto-reply gets interrupted more — nothing is covered, so everything escalates to their cell phone. The covered rep is the one who's actually off.

Urgent customer email
Auto-reply rep: a robot says “back Monday.” Covered rep: a human answers within hours.
Competitor circling the account
Auto-reply rep: open door, nobody home. Covered rep: the wingman holds the line.
Deal momentum mid-cycle
Auto-reply rep: stalls a week, restarts cold. Covered rep: keeps moving, handoff logged in Engage.
Your actual vacation
Auto-reply rep: interrupted by the “urgent” calls anyway. Covered rep: truly off — coverage has it.
Your One Move

Closers Don't
Go Dark

Delete the auto-reply today. Name your wingman before your next day off, and log the handoff in Engage. Your customers should never learn you were gone — only that FSG always answered.
