Before the hardware

How the savings stack

Lever one Run the lights fewer hours: occupancy and vacancy sensors, schedules, plug-load control.
Lever two Run them at lower wattage: task tuning and daylight harvesting trim what’s left.
Lever three Demand response — shed load when the grid needs it, and get paid to do it.
Combined Up to 60% of the existing lighting load — before a single fixture changes.

1 · Functionality

Decide how each space should behave before you pick a part number. Some rooms want motion or ambient-light sensors. Some want schedules — timers, cloud apps, or the building management system. Some just want a switch and a scene selector. The right answer is per space, not per catalog.

2 · Adjustment

How much control does the light itself need? On/off is the floor. From there: dimming, tunable white, full color-changing — or a combination. The occupant experience you’re promising sets this one.

3 · Connection

Wired controls are generally more reliable — and more expensive to retrofit. Wireless installs faster, fits existing buildings, and lets you regroup fixtures without pulling a single new wire. In retrofit work, that flexibility is often the whole ballgame.

4 · Power

Four ways to feed a control: line voltage (120V or 277V — lower upfront cost, handles heavy loads), low voltage (12V or 24V, needs a transformer), battery-operated (cheapest to install, but batteries become a maintenance line item), and kinetic — switches that power themselves from the press.

5 · Protocol

0-10V is one-way analog — simple and everywhere. DALI is two-way wired and can report driver status back. DMX runs theatrical and architectural color. Wireless brings Bluetooth mesh, Zigbee, Z-Wave, and Wi-Fi, each with trade-offs — Frank’s nod went to Bluetooth Low Energy, because nearly every phone and laptop in the building already speaks it.

6 · Scalability

Standalone controls are cost-effective for a single room — and tell you almost nothing. Networked controls span rooms, floors, and whole buildings, and hand back the analytics. Decide early which one you’re buying, because you can’t bolt a network onto standalone parts later.

7 · Tracking

A networked system is more than a light switch. It can feed energy management and ESG reporting, track tagged assets through the building, and show how space actually gets used through its occupancy sensing. The controls budget starts buying answers, not just light.

And Then: Pick Your Partner

Many of the biggest names in lighting have exited the market. Ask what happens to support if your vendor disappears — and be wary when the warranty is longer than the provider has been in business. In Frank’s words: “that’s not a good sign. Pick carefully.” Work with subject-matter experts who will still pick up the phone in year nine.
