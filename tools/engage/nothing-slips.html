Why — Memory Is Not a System

The most expensive sentence in sales is “I'll remember.” You won't — not at forty accounts, not at three hundred emails a week, not three quotes deep on a Thursday. Deals don't get lost to competitors nearly as often as they get lost to dropped balls: the spec nobody sent, the follow-up nobody made, the promise nobody wrote down. The fix isn't trying harder. It's a system that holds every commitment so your head doesn't have to.

Rule 1

Write It Down

The moment a commitment exists, it becomes a task. If it only lives in your head, it's already slipping.

Rule 2

Date It

A task without a due date is a wish. The date is what makes it show up in your morning — before it's late.

Rule 3

Link It

Tie every task to its account or opportunity. The task carries the deal's context, and the deal carries the proof of work.

The Multiplier

Assign It

Tasks aren't solo. Assign them to Solutions, Operations, or Leadership to pull support onto any account, opportunity, or estimate. Nothing slips — and we're all accountable to take action.

Your memory is for selling. The system is for remembering.
Ep 8 said your gut is the #1 talent of a salesperson. A gut that's busy guarding forty open loops can't read the room. Put the loops in Engage, and your full attention walks into every conversation — that's what the customer feels.

Create the task, assign it, @mention them in the description — it lands on their list with a due date, tied to your deal. One team, one record, every commitment owned.

How — Run the Play: The Nothing Slips Loop

This is the daily rhythm that makes the system hold — exactly as it's running on Reformation right now.

1

Start in the task view — before your inbox

The inbox is everyone else's agenda. The task view is yours. Ten minutes in Task Manager before the first email sets the whole day: what's due, what's slipping, what moves money.

2

Big Rock first — sort by deal impact, not noise

The first task you work is the one tied to the biggest deal, not the easiest checkbox. Noise feels productive. The Big Rock pays.

3

Capture it the moment it exists

Meeting wraps, email lands, call ends — every ask and every promise becomes a task with a due date before it can slip. Thirty seconds now beats an apology later. This is the Ep 7 and Ep 8 habit with a deadline on it.

4

Link every task to its deal

Parent type: Opportunity. Now the task opens with the whole story behind it — and the deal's Tasks tab shows the work to anyone who picks up the account.

5

Assign for support — Solutions · Operations · Leadership

Need a spec, a crew, a decision? Create the task, assign it to the teammate who owns the answer, @mention them in the description. On Reformation, that's the Tunable Lighting Control spec sitting on Cecil's list with a date — not floating in a hallway conversation.

6

Close the loop — and roll the thread

Done means marked Complete and the next action created, because a finished task that spawns nothing is a dropped thread. Date moved? Update it — a rolled task is honest, a stale one is invisible.

↻

Then sell with a clear head

The system holds the loops so your gut can do its real job: reading the customer, feeling the timing, making the call. Winners don't have better memories — they have better systems, and the system is now holding everything.

Do / Don't

Do this

  • Create the task while the commitment is still warm — in the meeting, not after it.
  • Give every task a date, a deal, and an owner — what, who, by when, on which opportunity.
  • Start every workday from the task view, Big Rock first.
  • Assign and @mention for support — Solutions, Operations, and Leadership are one task away.
  • Close the loop and roll the thread — Complete, then create what's next.

Not this

  • Use your inbox — or your memory — as the to-do list.
  • Leave a task undated. An undated task is invisible until it's too late.
  • Hoard the work. A task you should have assigned is a bottleneck with your name on it.
  • Knock out ten easy nothings while the Big Rock waits.
  • Mark Complete and walk away — if it owed a next step, the thread just died.
