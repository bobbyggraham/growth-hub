The Tale of Two Records

The session opened with two account records side by side. Same company size, same sector, same market. One record: every core field filled — named to standard, owner assigned, hierarchy set, contacts linked, activity logged. The other: a name and a blank stare. Then the question that ran the whole episode: which rep gets the callback? Which one wins the renewal?

You already know the answer. The record with the data is the record with the relationship — and the relationship is where the revenue is. Data quality = revenue. That's not a slogan; it's the mechanism this whole page walks through.

Anatomy of an Account Record — The Fields That Feed Your Number

Not every field is equal. These are the ones that drive reporting, rollups, and your commission:

1

The Name — To Standard, Every Time

A record nobody can find is a record nobody works. Name it to the FSG standard so search, reporting, and the duplicate screen all do their jobs — the full naming play runs in Built Right the First Time.

2

The Core Fields — What Reporting Reads

Sector, address, phone, website, class. Every blank field is a report that can't see the account, a rollup that undercounts it, and a commission run that can't route it. Five minutes here saves five hours downstream.

3

The Owner — Whose Kingdom Is It

Ownership decides whose pipeline the account feeds and who reporting credits. If an account you work isn't in My Accounts, don't shrug — run the Request Assignment play and make it official.

★

The Class — BA / MA / EA

Business, Market, or Enterprise — the class tells everyone how the account is covered and reviewed. Know where your accounts sit and what moves them: Account Class Defined is the two-minute read.

The Hierarchy — Parents, Children & Rollups

National accounts aren't one record — they're a family. Get the family tree right and the reporting tells the truth at every level:

LevelWhat it isWhy it matters
ParentThe national or corporate entity — think JLL or CBRE at the topWhere the relationship, the MSA, and the national numbers live. Leadership reads the account at this level.
ChildThe location, region, or property under the parentWhere the work actually happens — the site your crew rolls to and your opportunity attaches to.
The rollupChild activity summing to the parentAn opportunity parked on the wrong level, or a location floating outside the family, silently understates the whole relationship.
The rule.  Attach the work where the work happens — the child — and let the rollup carry it to the parent. An orphan location is revenue the national story never gets credit for.

Slice the Kingdom — Lists & Filters

A 78,000-row module is not a territory. A territory is a set of lists you actually work:

The sliceThe moveWhat it hands you
By sectorFilter your accounts on market sectorA call-block list that talks the same language all afternoon.
By sizeSort on revenue or location countWhere the whale accounts are — and which ones deserve an account plan.
By activitySort by Last Activity, oldest firstThe quiet corners of the kingdom — dormant accounts are found money.
Your bookPin My Accounts to the home viewThe whole territory at a glance, every morning, zero clicks.

Link the People — Build the Org Map Inside the Record

An account with no contacts is a building with no doors. Every contact linked to the account is a way in: the facilities manager who calls in the work, the procurement lead who signs it, the regional director who renews it. Link every contact you meet to the account — the org map you build inside the record is the map the next opportunity walks in on. And when a new name shows up in your inbox, remember Episode 1: search the person first — they may already be a lead or a contact.

Walk Your Kingdom — The Territory Checklist

0 of 6 — the map is still blank

Do / Don't

Do

  • Fill every core field the day you touch the account — five minutes, once.
  • Check the class (BA / MA / EA) and know what it means for coverage.
  • Attach opportunities at the child, and let the rollup tell the national story.
  • Work from lists — sector blocks, size sorts, last-activity sweeps.
  • Link every contact you meet. The org map is the account's real value.

Don't

  • Work an account that isn't assigned to you — request it and make it count.
  • Leave a location floating outside its parent. Orphans break the rollup.
  • Trust a bare record. Blank fields hide accounts from every report that matters.
  • Treat the module as your territory. Unfiltered rows are noise, not a book.
  • Let a quiet account stay quiet — dormant is a list, and lists get worked.
