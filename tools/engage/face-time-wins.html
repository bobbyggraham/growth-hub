Why — What Sales Was Called To Do

Look at the bottom of the Engage mark. Three words, in order: Chosen · Called · Sent. That's not decoration. It's the job description, and it comes from John 9 and 10.

Everything on this page — the three roles, the meeting, the account plan — is just that order played out on one account. Empowering people to do good work.

Chosen→Called→Sent
John 9 · 1

Chosen

“…so that the works of God might be displayed in him.” — John 9:3

A man born blind. The disciples ask whose fault it was. Jesus says it isn't about fault at all — this man was chosen for this moment. In John 10 He says it plainly: His flock doesn't belong to the world. They were given to Him.

For us: you weren't handed an account by accident. You were put on it. Act like the person who was picked for this one.

John 10 · 2

Called

“He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out… they know his voice.” — John 10:3–4

The Good Shepherd doesn't herd. He calls by name, and the sheep follow because they recognize the voice. When the healed man gets thrown out of the synagogue, Jesus goes and finds him, and he follows.

For us: the customer follows the voice they know. Wayne Covington answered 46 review notes in one day because he knows ours. That's the relationship a Guide builds, buddy to buddy, before anyone talks price.

John 9 · 3

Sent

“Go, wash in the Pool of Siloam” (this word means “Sent”). — John 9:7

Mud on the eyes and one instruction: go. The pool is literally named Sent, and the One who sends him was Himself sent. He obeys, he sees, and he's immediately out in a hostile room testifying: “I was blind but now I see.”

For us: you're sent into the account. Do the work in the open, then testify — the proof card, the logged meeting, the win on the board. That's the testimony.

The whole episode in one line: Chosen for the account. Called by name, so the customer knows your voice. Sent to do the work and bring back the testimony. Contribution + Service = Value.

One Account, Three Roles, One Team

Protect · Land · Expand isn't one person's job. It's three roles handing the same account forward, each one carrying a level. The handoffs are where accounts get lost — and the handoffs are exactly what the Engage Meeting is for.

Level 1

The Guide

Human engagement · buddy to buddy

The first voice the customer knows. No pitch. Shows up, listens, helps, and keeps showing up.

  • Runs the 5-Star Lead test on every relationship: Willing to Engage · Friendly and Collaborative · Knows What They Want · Knows When They Want It · Knows They Want It From YOU.
  • Logs every meeting, even the ones with no deal attached. That's the account story starting.
  • Transition trigger: five stars lit → hands to Level 2 with the record complete.
On Reformation Years of buddy-to-buddy with Wayne's team — which is why a rep change didn't cost us the account.
Level 2

Business Development

Sower and Closer · Land & Expand

Sows new ground and closes it. Lands the first category, then expands into the next one inside the same account, or rescues an at-risk account.

  • Owns the face-to-face and the account plan that comes out of it.
  • Opens the new opportunities: new categories, new sites, the fixed-price program.
  • Builds the team around the account — AM, Ops, Finance — in the internal meeting.
  • Transition trigger: account built out, customer says it's healthy → hands to Level 3.
On Reformation Bobby leads, Donn Koonce supports, through the Ref 3.0 build-out. Six new opportunities opened off the Sept 30 meeting.
Level 3

Account Manager

Protect · Manage · Enrich

Keeps what was won and makes it worth more. The customer's one voice at FSG for the long haul.

  • Protect: every open action item closed in writing. No surprises, no stale records.
  • Manage: the cadence — daily swings, weekly clear-the-bases, monthly check-your-average.
  • Enrich: the contact map grows, the plan gets re-versioned, service turns into a program.
  • Shadows from week one so the handoff is invisible to the customer.
On Reformation Tiffany Santiago (Strategic Accounts) takes ownership at the QBR, with Wayne in the room. Date set there, not before.
The rule: a level is a job on the account, not a rank. One rep can carry all three on a small account. On a $1.5M pipeline with 23 open opportunities, it takes a team — and the team has to meet.

How — Run the Play: The Internal Meeting After the Face-to-Face

The customer meeting is where you listen. The internal meeting is where the team turns what you heard into an account plan, an action list, and a clean record. Here's the sequence, exactly as it ran on Reformation.

1

Face-to-face with the customer — log it within 24 hours

Sept 30: Bobby meets Wayne Covington. Before the day ends, the meeting is on the Reformation account in Engage: who was there, what they said, what we owe them, and the nine action items. Nothing lives in a notebook.

2

Schedule the internal meeting from the opportunity

On the live opportunity, create Team – Account Planning Session. Invite the team the account needs: BD (Bobby, Donn), the incoming AM (Tiffany), Ops (Leon on supply, Lindsey on the group inbox), lighting design (David). Add the join link. Everyone sees it on their Dashboard.

3

Notes: build the account header first

Top of the notes, one table, no prose: ERP ID · Account Level · FSG Owner path (lead → support → AM at handoff) · Engage codes · Engage snapshot (open opps, active $, 2025 sales, 2026 YTD) · Lumen desk · Drive folder · Updated date. Then link the Account Plan (v1.2) and Open in Engage. The next person reads this in 20 seconds.

4

Notes: the stakeholder map, as the customer gave it to you

Reformation's org, by department and function: Retail Development (Pre-construction, Creative, Construction) and Facilities (Corporate & Retail, Retail). Names and roles exactly as Wayne supplied them, with spelling variances flagged. Work Retail Dev to Land; work Facilities to Expand.

5

Notes → Tasks: every action item gets an owner and a date

The Action List – Transition in the notes (org chart, stakeholders, APEX access, bulk pricing, design review, tunable programming, inventory reserve, group email, standup) becomes nine Tasks. Owner, due date, done. The Dashboard does the nagging so you don't have to.

6

Attachments + Summary: close the loop

Attach the Account Plan PDF and the design-review markup. Write the Summary: where the account is, the next move, who owns it, when the handoff is. Then open the six new opportunities the plan calls for — one Delivery Method, one Product, a live follow-up date, a future close date, value at least $1.

↻

Then the cadence — base hits win championships

Daily (5 min): any Reformation record at the top of the Dashboard gets its follow-up date, close date, and value. Weekly (Friday, 15 min): every open opportunity has a live follow-up date; every transition item updated to Wayne in writing. Monthly (first Monday): walk every record, replace $1 placeholders with real pricing, re-version the plan.

Meeting Notes Your Future Self Can Use

A meeting note that says “good meeting, follow up next week” is worth nothing in eight months. One that reads like this is worth a renewal:

The note that wins — five lines, every time
WhoNames and roles, theirs and ours. Who decided, who influenced, who was quiet.
What they saidIn their words. “We want one thermostat standard” beats “discussed HVAC.” This is what the proof card is built from later.
What we oweEvery promise FSG made, with a date. Wayne turned 46 notes in a day — we answer in 48 hours, in writing.
What they oweWhat the customer said they'd send: the fleet list, the 2027 pipeline, the sub list by market.
Next action + dateOne task, one owner, one date — created in the Tasks tab before you close the meeting.
Link it to the opportunity. A meeting attached to an opportunity is activity the stage can see. A meeting floating on the account is a diary entry.

Do / Don't

Do this

  • Log the meeting the same day, in the customer's words.
  • Build the internal meeting off the opportunity, not in a text thread.
  • Put the account header at the top of the notes so the next person is oriented in 20 seconds.
  • Turn every action item into a Task with a date.
  • Bring the incoming AM into the meeting from week one.

Not this

  • “Good meeting, will follow up.”
  • Notes in Apple Notes, Outlook, or your head.
  • A meeting with no attendees and no join link — nobody shows up to a ghost.
  • Action items that live only in the notes. If it isn't a Task, it doesn't get done.
  • Handing an account off with no meeting record behind it.
