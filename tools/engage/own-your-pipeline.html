The Closer's Creed — No. 10
“Your pipeline is your paycheck's rough draft. Edit it weekly.”
Last week — Nothing Slips: every commitment became a task — written down, dated, linked, assigned. Tasks keep the promises. This week we zoom out to the thing the promises add up to: the account plan. Tasks are what you’re doing. The plan tells you what’s done and what’s next — and “next” is where pipeline comes from.

Why — Pipeline Starts at the Account

Most reps “manage pipeline” by scrolling a list of opportunities and nudging close dates. That’s editing the last page of the book. The money is decided earlier — in how well you know the account: what we’ve already delivered, what’s in flight, who decides, who on our side is working it, and what we should be opening next. Own that, and the pipeline isn’t something you groom. It’s something you grow.

Done · Doing · Next.
Three words, every account, every week. Done is your proof. Doing is your promise. Next is your paycheck.

And it connects back to Ep 7: Guide, Business Development, and Account Manager working one account as a team on Protect · Land · Expand. The account plan is where that team keeps score.

The Opening Hook

Pick any opportunity on the screen and ask three questions: Who are the stakeholders? Who on our side is assigned to it? What’s the next opportunity on that account? Give it ten seconds. “If you can’t answer all three, you don’t own that pipeline — you’re renting it. Let’s buy it.”

How — Run the Play: The Weekly Plan Walk

Twenty minutes, once a week, top accounts first. This is how you edit the rough draft.

1

Open the account plan, not the opportunity list

Start where the money is decided. Top accounts first — the ones that pay you most or could.

2

Move what’s Done

Won work and completed tasks go to Done. That’s your proof — and your wedge for what’s next.

3

Pressure-test what’s Doing

Every open opportunity: would you bet your commission on this stage and this close date? Every live task: owner, date, deal. Fix it while you’re looking at it.

4

Check the team

Key stakeholders named, additional stakeholders mapped, internal support team assigned. Missing someone? That’s a Next item.

5

Refresh the Deal Coach

Take the wedge, the questions, and the opportunities to open. Turn the next action into a task with a date — Ep 9 rules.

★

Open one Next opportunity — from the contact

Go to the stakeholder who owns the need and open it there. Engage links the account, contact, and owner. Next just became Doing — and your pipeline just grew.

Do / Don't

Do this

  • Walk your top account plans every week — Done, Doing, Next.
  • Open new opportunities from the contact, so the stakeholder is on the deal from day one.
  • Name our support team on the account — nobody sells alone.
  • Turn every Deal Coach next action into a task with a date.

Not this

  • Manage pipeline from the opportunity list alone — that’s editing the last page.
  • Leave Next empty — an empty Next is a shrinking account.
  • Run a whole account through one contact.
  • Create an opportunity from scratch and link it later — let Engage do it.
