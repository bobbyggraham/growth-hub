The Closer's Creed — No. 8
“Your inbox is full of money. Log it.”
Last week — Face Time Wins: we logged the meeting, wrote the five-line note, and turned action items into tasks. That wasn't housekeeping. Every meeting and note you logged was a deposit — and this week, the record you built starts paying you back. The Deal Coach reads all of it.

Why — Communication Is the Deal

Ask any customer why they left a vendor. It's almost never price. It's silence. The quote went out and nobody followed up. The project hit a snag and nobody said so. The deal closed and nobody came back. Regular communication cadence is the key to income — the deal moves at the speed of the conversation, and the conversation mostly happens in email.

Move 1

Follow Up

Every quote, every meeting, every ask gets an answer with a date on it. The follow-up is the sale.

Move 2

Update Along the Way

No news is news. “Still on track, pricing lands Thursday” costs you 20 seconds and buys you trust.

Move 3

A Reason to Come Back

Every touch carries something: the login that now works, the list they asked for, the next date. Never just “checking in.”

The Governor

Presence & Patience

Not too much. Just in time. Enough that they never wonder where you went — never so much they wince at your name.

Your gut is the #1 talent of a salesperson.
Engage doesn't replace it — it feeds it. When every call, email, note, and meeting is on the record, your gut is working with the whole story, not the half you remember. That's what this episode builds.
Build as you go. Nobody sits down and “does CRM.” You log the call when you hang up. You pin the email when it lands. You drop the note when it's fresh. Thirty seconds at a time, the record builds itself — and the coach gets smarter with every brick.

How — Run the Play: The Cadence Loop

This is the week-in, week-out rhythm that turns an inbox into income — exactly as it's running on Reformation right now.

1

Work from the record — click to call, click to email

Start on the opportunity, not in your inbox. The Call and Email buttons at the top of the record mean the touch starts logged. One click, and the conversation is already part of the deal.

2

Email lands → pin it to the opportunity

Customer replies, forwards, or asks something new? Open the opportunity's Emails tab, run Find Related Emails (or let auto-match surface it), and pin the thread. Thirty seconds, and the whole team — including the next owner of this account — can see it.

3

New ask inside a thread → capture it before it slips

When a pinned thread carries a new ask — “can you also price the tunable controls?” — that's not a reply, that's an opportunity. Open it while the email is still warm: one Delivery Method, one Product, a live follow-up date, value at least $1. Inbox to income, literally.

4

Feed the record after every touch

Call ends, meeting wraps, email sends — drop the note, create the task, set the follow-up date. This is the Ep 7 habit doing double duty: it keeps the team aligned and it's the fuel the Deal Coach runs on.

5

Deal Coach → Suggested Email → send the follow-up

When it's time to go back to the customer, open Deal Coach. The Suggested Email already carries your open items — the APEX login, the location lists, the group meeting ask. Trim it to your voice, send it, and log what comes back. On Reformation, that one email closes out three open commitments.

6

Deal moved? Refresh the coaching

Meeting logged, task closed, new thread pinned — hit Refresh coaching. The coach re-reads the record and re-draws the next move. A Deal Coach you never refresh is a newspaper from last month.

↻

Then trust the gut — presence and patience

The system tells you what's open and when you last touched. Your gut tells you when — when to nudge, when to wait, when to pick up the phone instead. Not too much, just in time. That timing is the #1 talent of a salesperson, and now it's working with the full record.

Do / Don't

Do this

  • Start the call or email from the record — one click, already logged.
  • Pin the threads that carry the deal: asks, commitments, pricing, dates.
  • Send updates before they're asked for — “still on track” is a selling email.
  • Give every touch a reason — something delivered, something answered, a next date.
  • Refresh the Deal Coach after every milestone, and read it before every follow-up.

Not this

  • “Just checking in” with nothing in your hands.
  • Pin everything — a record full of noise is as blind as an empty one.
  • Let the quote age in silence because “they said they'd get back to us.”
  • Send the Suggested Email without reading it. AI drafts, you decide — it's your name on it.
  • Carpet-bomb the customer. Presence and patience — just in time beats all the time.
