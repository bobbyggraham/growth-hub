Account Class Defined

How FSG classifies accounts as Business, Market, or Enterprise — the three tests, who gets paid, and how accounts move between classes.
FACILITY SOLUTIONS GROUP
Growth Organization  ·  Effective 2026

Why This Matters

Account class decides who gets paid, who owns the relationship, and how we resource it. The class isn't about how big an account looks — it's about what is actually driving the revenue and how much coordination it takes to serve. Three classes, three clear tests.

Apply the test, not the gut feel. If you can name what drives the revenue, you can name the class.

Business Account (BA)

Revenue arrives on its own. A Business (house) account is one where the sale happens without a rep driving it — inbound, repeat, or order-taking. No one is actively selling it, so there's no one to pay.

Qualifies as Business when —

  • Revenue is inbound, repeat, or order-taking that doesn't depend on active selling.
  • No one is formally assigned to grow the relationship.
  • Core clients to Construction and Offices they've developed, managed, and executed well — clients who don't need a sales representative.

Market Account (MA)

One rep drives it, one rep can cover it. A Market account has a named, approved owner whose work generates the revenue, and the account is simple enough that one person can serve it well.

Qualifies as Market when —

  • It has a formally assigned, account manager–approved owner who produces trackable opportunities within the FSG Apps: Engage, Services Labs, and Construction Labs.
  • The owner actively calls on the account, renews it, and grows it over the previous year.
  • It's single-site or single-region, or runs through one local decision-maker.
  • It's typically one business line — or buying that doesn't need cross-line coordination.

Enterprise Account (EA)

The value is in coordination, so it earns one quarterback. An Enterprise account starts with the same foundation as Market — an approved owner actively working the account — and adds scale. It qualifies when it meets two of the three scale tests below.

The foundation — always required

  • A formally assigned, account manager–approved owner who generates trackable opportunities within the FSG Apps: Engage, Services Labs, and Construction Labs.
  • The owner actively calls on the account, provides a quarterly business review and account plan, manages the budget, renews the account, and grows it year over year.

The scale tests — meet two of three

1

Cross-Line Scope

Buys across multiple FSG business lines — or has clear, leadership-backed potential to.

2

Multi-Site Footprint

A portfolio of locations spanning multiple regions.

3

Revenue

Annual revenue or qualified 12-month potential at or above the Enterprise threshold.

Leadership override. The Chief Growth Officer (or designated leadership) can name an account Enterprise for strategic reasons, regardless of the metrics. Overrides are tracked as an explicit, named list, so the exception is always visible and intentional — not a backdoor around the test.

Movement Between Classes

Accounts move up and down. The rules below keep reclassification fair and prevent surprise payouts on revenue a rep didn't create.

Moving up

↑

Business → Market

Converts when a formally assigned, approved owner takes it on. Commission applies going forward only — no backdated credit on existing house revenue.

↑

Market → Enterprise

Re-tiers at review when it crosses the scope and revenue tests. Credit handling for a mid-year promotion is determined before the first promotion, not after.

Moving down

↓

Market → Business

If the active selling stops and revenue reverts to inbound/repeat, the account returns to house at the next review. The former owner keeps the credit earned while actively selling — but not on revenue that continues without them.

↓

Enterprise → Market

If it no longer needs cross-line or multi-site coordination (e.g., consolidates to a single site or line), it steps down to Market with a single owner. Handle in-flight credit before the change.

Large unsolicited orders

A large unsolicited order on a Business account triggers a review for coverage — not an automatic reclassification and not a retroactive payout. The question is whether the account now warrants an owner going forward, not whether someone gets paid for an order that walked in the door.

Review Cadence

Classifications are reviewed quarterly by Sales Operations with CGO sign-off. Reps can flag an account for re-tiering any time, but changes take effect at the review — with credit handling agreed in advance — so timing never drives the outcome.
