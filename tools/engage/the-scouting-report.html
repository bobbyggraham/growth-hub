Why — Film Work Wins Game Day

Every skill in Season One was a single position. The Scouting Report is the whole team on the field. Teams that win on Saturday don’t get lucky on Saturday — they did the film work on Tuesday. A scouting report is how you walk into the meeting already knowing what we protect, where we land, and how we expand — and which threats could take it all away.

Protect

What we already do here. The revenue we can’t afford to lose — and who could take it.

Land

The open deals. Honest stage, honest date, a stakeholder on every one.

Expand

The whitespace. New lines, new sites, new stakeholders — the Next column.

The Threat Assessment

Every scouting report names what can beat us. Three things to look for on the tape:

1

Competitor presence

Who else is on site? Which service lines are going to somebody else — and why?

2

Contract timing

What renews, rebids, or expires in the next twelve months? A date nobody tracked is a door somebody else walks through.

3

Relationship gaps

One contact is a relationship; three is an account. If our champion left tomorrow, who picks up the phone?

Do / Don't

Do this

  • Scout before every big meeting — film work first.
  • Name the threats out loud. What you name, you can beat.
  • Log the three moves in Engage before you close the report.
  • Brief it in five minutes. If it takes fifteen, you don’t know it yet.

Not this

  • Walk in on memory and hope.
  • Build a report nobody acts on — a scouting report without moves is a scrapbook.
  • Scout on a broken record — fix the account first.
  • Keep it to yourself — share it with your manager and your support team.
