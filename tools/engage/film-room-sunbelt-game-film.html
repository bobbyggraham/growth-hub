Pre-GameSeason One — Master the Tool · Episodes 1–11
Where we are in the seasonMission Control gave you the dashboard. Know Your Territory gave you the account record. The Film Room is where both get put to work — a structured review of one strategic account. Full lineup lives in the Seasons 1 & 2 Training Guide.

1 · The Game Plan — Start With The Plan, Not The App

A film session without a game plan is just watching TV. Before you touch a tab, read the account plan — it tells you what you're looking for. Sunbelt's plan is on the hub: Sunbelt Rentals — Account Plan.

The plan in one breath: Protect the reciprocal trade we've earned. Land one DFW branch with a no-charge lighting & power audit. Expand into ~1,180 stores untouched by their 2025 LED program plus 300–400 greenfield depots — seven opportunities, five plays, one owner. Everything on the tape below either advances that plan or exposes a gap in it.

2 · The Scoreboard — Opportunities

Frame 01Account → Opportunities tab
What to watchThe four scoreboard cards read the account in five seconds: 33 active opportunities, $26.5M in active opportunity dollars, and 2026 sales YTD at $1.6M against $122K for all of 2025 — a 13× jump. That's what a worked account looks like. Then read the grid the way a coach reads the line of scrimmage: Stage, Est. Value, Follow-Up, Bid Due, Est. Close.
Freeze it right thereTwo rows in the frame have an empty Est. Close date — including a $243K solar deal sitting in Pending Customer with a follow-up date of 06/15 that's already behind us. And there are $1.00 values still riding along. The rules from Episode 2 apply on every touch: follow-up never blank, close date never in the past, value never $0 — and the $1 placeholder is a bridge, not a home. In the Film Room, a blank cell is an opening: it tells you exactly what your next touch is.

3 · The Roster — Contacts

Frame 02Account → Contacts tab
What to watchThree names on the roster — two branch managers and a sustainability manager, each tagged with a Job Function (Influencer, Buyer). Now compare that to the game plan: the account plan's contact map names eight targets across program, regional, branch, and trade tiers. Five people who decide this account's future aren't in our system yet.
Whitespace isn't just products — it's people. The gap between the plan's contact map and this tab IS the week's call sheet. And honor the flags while you work it: on this account, Bryan Barham is never dialed — both numbers carry DNC. Email or in person only.

4 · The Paper — A/R Status

Frame 03Account → General → A/R Status
What to watchAP contact on file (Jay Badger), Master Account bill-to in Charlotte, Net 30 terms, surcharges set — and the stat most reps never scroll to: average days to pay: 2. Sunbelt pays in two days. When Operations asks whether this customer is worth mobilizing for, that number is the answer.

Why this tab is in the film session: deals die in activation, not just in selling. A complete A/R Status is what lets a Closed Won turn into a Work Order without a week of email. If any of this is blank on your account, run the Account Activation Request play before you need it.

5 · The Depth Chart — Master Rep & Site Assignments

Frame 04Account → General → Basic Information
What to watchTeam Details is the depth chart. Account Class: Enterprise Account. Division: (90) National Accounts. Account Portfolio Manager: Kayte Bachyrycz — the master rep, the one name accountable for the whole relationship. The assign icon beside that field is Click to Assign: ownership on this account is one click, not one email thread.
Frame 05Account → Sites tab
What to watchOne master rep up top, but every site row carries its own Account Manager — that's how a 1,611-store national account gets covered without anyone owning everything. The column filter is open in the frame: filter Account Manager for blanks and "Open," and you've got the unclaimed territory. Spot an open site in your market? That's Click to Assign again — claim it, or run the Request Assignment flow.
Site attribution is where credit lives. Work at a site flows to the rep assigned to that site. An unassigned site isn't just messy data — it's commission nobody has claimed and a customer location nobody is watching.

6 · The Two-Minute Drill — Lead to Account, Done Right

Now the highlight reel. A Tier 1 target from the Sunbelt plan — Karen Beadle, SVP of Sustainability, the executive who owns the LED program and the Net Zero mandate — goes from a business card to a clean, converted, AI-briefed record. Watch how little typing happens.

Frame 06Leads → Upload Business Card
What to watchClick to Capture. Upload Business Card takes a photo or a screenshot — here it's reading Karen's full target block: title, account, email, mobile, LinkedIn, even the play call. Snap it in the parking lot before you start the truck.
Frame 07The scan → the record it builds
What to watchTen seconds of "Still scanning…" and out comes a structured record: Karen Beadle — SVP, Sustainability, Fort Mill corporate, verified email, mobile with no DNC flag — cleared to call, in seat since June 2023, ZoomInfo accuracy 95. The Play/Next Action line even carries her assignment from the account plan: Plays 1 and 5, call directly.
Frame 08Create Lead — pre-filled
What to watchThe Create Lead form arrives already filled — name, company, title, phone, email. Your job is the two fields the scan can't know: Owner (the rep who works it) and Division. Save as New Lead and it's in the system, spelled right, thirty seconds after the handshake.
Then hit Enrich. The ZoomInfo panel opens beside the lead with a full match: it flags what's New (mobile, job function, management level) and what Differs from what we typed — with a copy button per field. Nothing touches your record until you copy it across, so you stay in control of the data. Thirty more seconds and the lead is verified against a live B2B database instead of a guess.
Frame 09AI Brief — your pre-call prep, written for you
What to watchOne click and the AI Brief reads everything on the record — notes, history, intent — and hands you the call sheet: who she is, the ~1,180-store Phase 2 potential, the 99 intent score on LED lighting, and the recommended next step. This is the ten-minute pre-meeting prep from the field guide, compressed into the time it takes to pour coffee. Generated by AI — review it before you dial.
Frame 10Convert Lead → New Account
What to watchKaren answered, the meeting is set — time to convert. The Convert Lead flow drafts the account from what the lead already knows: Sunbelt Rentals, Fort Mill address, phone. Hit Next — and don't touch Save yet, because the next screen is the whole ballgame.
Frame 11Select Account Duplicate — the play that saves the season
What to watchEngage checks before it creates: two exact matches on Sunbelt Rentals — ERP 737778 (the National Accounts record with 33 live opportunities) and 108626 — plus a page of fuzzy near-misses (Sunbelt Pools, Sunbelt Mill Supply…). The right move is selected in the frame: attach to 737778, the account we just spent this whole film session inside.
The turnover you never throw"Save as New Account" on a national account is a fumble at the goal line. A duplicate Sunbelt splits the pipeline, hides the $26.5M from whoever opens the other record, orphans the site assignments, and makes two reps think they own the same customer. When exact matches show, you attach — you only save new when nothing real matches. Ten seconds of reading this screen protects years of account history.

The Do / Don't Board

Run it like this

  • Open the account plan first — review with a purpose
  • Read the scoreboard cards, then hunt blank fields like openings
  • Compare the Contacts tab to the plan's contact map — the gap is your call sheet
  • Check A/R before you promise Operations anything
  • Claim or assign open sites the moment you spot them
  • Capture the card → Enrich → AI Brief → then dial
  • Attach converted leads to the existing account when Engage finds a match

Not like this

  • Review an account by scrolling and vibes — no plan, no findings
  • Leave a blank Est. Close or a stale $1 you noticed — a spotted gap is your task
  • Cold-dial a DNC-flagged contact because the mobile was right there
  • Retype a business card by hand and invent a new spelling of the company
  • Skip the Enrich panel and let a guessed title ride into the CRM
  • Save as New Account past two exact matches — that's the turnover
