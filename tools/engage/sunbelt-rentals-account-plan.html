The Window Is Open Right Now

Sunbelt is shopping, today. ZoomInfo shows LED lighting intent at score 99 with audience strength A, refreshed September 5, 2026. Planned maintenance is also at 99, energy storage at 94, exterior signage and channel letters at 91. Four of the five things FSG sells to a national footprint are lighting up at once.

And there's a gap worth walking through. In June 2025 Sunbelt completed a National LED Lighting Program across roughly 425 locations in 45 states, run by Chateau Energy Solutions after a competitive RFP. It delivered $1.73M in annual energy savings, $501,859 in rebates, and a 6,012 metric ton CO2 reduction. Sunbelt operates 1,611 stores. Roughly 1,180 have not been touched — and Sunbelt 4.0 adds 300 to 400 greenfield depots by 2029.

The business case is already written — by their own Phase 1. FSG doesn't have to sell the idea. FSG has to prove execution at scale.

The FSG Wedge

Chateau Energy is an energy program manager — audits, design, rebate capture, program administration.

FSG is the self-performing national contractor that installs, energizes, services, and maintains.

FSG does not have to displace the incumbent to win. FSG wins the execution layer on the next 1,180 stores, the greenfield build program, and everything electrical that a lighting-only program leaves on the table.

Expansion and capital triggers

  • 300–400 greenfield depots through 2029 — 180–240 specialty locations and 120–160 general tool stores. New construction electrical, lighting, yard lighting, and signage on every one.
  • Cluster density strategy — 21 of the top 25 U.S. markets are already clustered; the next 75 markets grow from 35 clusters to as many as 50. DFW is mature, which means more sites per FSG truck roll.
  • ~1,180 stores outside the 2025 LED program — the Phase 2 runway, supported by the live LED intent signal.
  • U.S. redomiciliation — now a U.S.-listed, U.S.-domiciled reporting company. Vendor consolidation and domestic supplier optics both favor a national U.S. contractor.
  • LA28 Olympic and Paralympic partnership — official rental equipment solutions and services partner. Temporary power, event lighting, and staging demand outside the store network.
  • DFW hiring activity — open Branch Manager postings in Irving and Canutillo as of late August 2026. Local movement usually means local site work.
  • Specialty growth — Power and HVAC, Climate Control, and Flooring expand fastest and carry the heaviest electrical requirements per site.

Entry Play — Bryan Barham

Bryan is the right first door, and he is not the decision maker for a national program. Play him as the proof-of-execution wedge.

  • Lead with the yard, not the boardroom. A branch manager is measured on uptime, safety, and utilization. Talk lighting levels in the yard after dark, gate and fence power, lift charging circuits, and how fast FSG shows up when something trips.
  • Speak service manager to service manager. Bryan ran regional service operations at H&E. He judges FSG on response time, first-time fix rate, and paperwork — not on a capabilities deck.
  • Respect the veteran and the operator. Direct, no fluff, specifics over adjectives. Bring a one-page scope, not a brochure.
  • Ask him who owns the store standard. The most valuable thing Bryan can give FSG is his District Manager's name and how corporate facilities capital gets requested. That's the bridge to Fort Mill.
  • Give him a reason to say yes small. One walkthrough, one lighting and power audit, no charge, one page of findings. Low risk for him — and it becomes the reference site.
The opening angle. Sunbelt just finished LED upgrades at about 425 locations. If PC 271 wasn't in that group, FSG walks the yard and shop and hands over a one-page findings sheet — what the upgrade would save, which rebates the site qualifies for — and Bryan decides from there. If the site was included, the conversation is about what a lighting-only program didn't cover: yard power, gate circuits, signage, and how fast an electrician shows up when something goes down.

Risks and Watch-Outs

  • Incumbent in place. Chateau Energy won the 2025 program through competitive RFP and delivered well publicly. Position FSG as execution, not as a replacement, until there's a reason to compete head-on.
  • Post-listing scrutiny. A newly U.S.-listed company under margin-expansion pressure will demand hard payback math. Every proposal states rebate capture, payback period, and GHG contribution up front.
  • Mixed construction market. Management describes end markets as mixed, with rental revenue guidance of 0–4%. Capital discretion is tight — self-funding energy projects clear faster than discretionary spend.
  • Contact data confidence. Bryan's ZoomInfo accuracy score is 50 and last refreshed November 2025. Confirm he's still in seat before anything goes into Engage as verified.
  • Do not cold call. Both of Bryan's numbers carry DNC flags. A cold dial is the fastest way to lose this door.
  • The $1 that never grows. Seven placeholder values on one account is a forecast that reads as empty. Every one of them is a task, not a decision.
