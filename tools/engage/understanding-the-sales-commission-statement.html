Understanding the Sales Commission Statement

How to read your monthly statement in Engage, what WIP and Earned mean for your pay, and your role in the approval cycle that gets you paid.
FACILITY SOLUTIONS GROUP
Growth Organization  ·  2026

Why This Matters

Your commission statement is your scoreboard — and your paycheck. It lives in Engage (engage.fsgapps.com → Commission Statements). This guide shows you how to read it, what WIP and Earned mean for your pay, what your role is in the monthly approval cycle, and the definitions behind every term you’ll see.

Five minutes a month protects every dollar you’ve earned. Review every line, every statement, every month.

Reading Your Commission Statement

The Summary (Top of Statement)

The summary shows your year-to-date totals and a month-by-month breakdown across six categories:

CategoryWhat it means
SalesThe total top line sales value of commissionable transactions.
Comm. Earned & WIPThe combined value of commission earned on all transactions regardless of transaction “status”.
Comm. ContestedThe sum of commission earned on transactions actively contested by the sales rep.
Salary PaidSalary that was paid to you for the period shown in the column header.
Comm. PaidCommission actually paid out to you.
Comm. DueCommission owed but not yet paid — earned commission minus what has been paid.

The Transactions (Detail)

Each line is one invoice. Key columns:

ColumnWhat it means
Invoice Date / Sale PeriodWhen the transaction was invoiced and the commission period it posts to.
Account ClassBA, EA, or MA — the customer’s account tier (see Account Class Definitions below).
Commission TypeThe plan and rate applied to the line (e.g., Type 7 – 3% of Sales).
Mgr. Approval / Rep ApprovalThe two approval gates — your manager’s and yours. Both are required before commission is paid.
StatusWIP or Earned (see WIP vs. Earned below).
NGPNet gross profit on the line — sales minus direct and indirect costs.
NotesComments attached to the line during review.
Drill into a line — the Invoice # is a live link. Click it to open the record behind the line — the Job, Work Order, or Order Entry — for a full deep dive. Use it to confirm sales, cost, NGP, or anything else about the transaction before you approve or contest the line.

WIP vs. Earned

StatusDefinition
WIPThe job, WO, or OE is not closed / posted, so it hits the commission statement as WIP (work in progress). You still earn commission on these at the typical rates, though it is common to see multiple invoices for a single job, WO, or OE — typically one invoice for each period the transaction is open.
EarnedThe job, WO, or OE is complete and no changes to the total order value or your commissions should be expected; those are essentially locked in.
✓

Multiple Invoices Are Normal

A single job, WO, or OE often generates one invoice per period it remains open.

−

Negative Lines Are Normal

WIP corrections can reduce prior commission — a negative amount is correction activity, not an error.

$

Large WIP Amounts

WIP commission is approved for payment, but large or at-risk amounts may be held until a future period until the revenue settles.

Account Class Definitions

CodeClassDefinition
BABusiness AccountA customer account owned and managed by a local salesperson or branch, typically serving a single location or regional customer.
EAEnterprise AccountA large multi-site customer account managed at the national level by the enterprise sales team, with centralized pricing, contracts, and relationship ownership.
MAMarket AccountA customer account managed at the market or regional level, larger than a local business account but not centralized under enterprise sales.

Your Role in the Monthly Approval Cycle

Each month’s statement undergoes a two-stage approval process on a fixed calendar. Your manager reviews first; then it’s your turn.

DaysWhat you do
10–14Your Sales Manager reviews the statement, tags and resolves issues, and submits. Nothing required from you yet.
15–19Your review window. Open your statement in Engage, review every line, tag “Contest” on anything that looks wrong (with a description), and submit your approval.
20+Records with both Mgr and Rep approval are submitted as Commission Due and sent to payroll. HR issues payment per the statement.

What to Check During Your Review

1

Account Class

Is the customer tiered correctly (BA / EA / MA)?

2

Account Rep / Site Rep

Are you credited on your transactions?

3

CX Rate and CX Basis

Is the right commission rate applied to the right basis?

If a line is correct, approve it. If something is wrong, tag the record “Contest” and add notes describing the problem. The record is returned to your manager to resolve, rebuilt automatically, and returned to you for approval. Contested records will not advance — and will not pay — until resolved and approved.

Approval deadline — act before it passes. Each statement must be approved by its posted deadline. Statements not approved by the deadline are considered approved by default and are no longer open for revision. If you don’t review, you lose the window to contest a line — review every month.
