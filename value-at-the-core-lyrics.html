The Lyrics — Decoded

Each section is tagged with the StoryBrand role it plays. This is why the anthem works as a pitch: it's a complete story in 90 seconds.

The Character — The Hero
It starts with a person carrying a problem they can't solve alone. They are the hero; we are their Guide. Growth is the way.
Why this works: every story starts with a character who wants something. Notice who the hero is — not FSG. The customer. The first word of our story belongs to them, and FSG takes the Guide's seat on line two. That single choice is the whole StoryBrand playbook in miniature.
The Problem — The Villain
Facility leaders are stuck — juggling too many vendors, blown schedules, dark sites, and bleeding budgets. Nobody is accountable. They need the light we bring.
Why this works: companies sell solutions to external problems; customers buy solutions to internal ones. "Blown schedules and dark sites" are the external problem. "Stuck" and "nobody is accountable" are the internal frustration — the feeling that actually drives the buying decision. Name the villain (fragmentation, zero accountability) and the hero leans in.
The Guide — Empathy + Authority
FSG is the one partner that self-performs nationally, built to solve facility problems. Local Strength, Nationwide Excellence — with People doing Good Work.
Why this works: a Guide earns trust two ways — empathy (we understand your problem) and authority (we're competent to solve it). "Built to solve facility problems" is empathy; "self-performs nationally" is authority no aggregator can claim. The Guide doesn't say "we're the best" — the Guide proves capability and hands the hero the win.
The Plan + Success
We discover the problem, bring the plan, do the work in the open, and success goes on and on and on. People, Vision, Development, Execution, Finance, Results. We focus on facilities across the globe.
Why this works: heroes don't follow guides without a path. "Discover → plan → work in the open" is a three-step plan a customer can repeat back after hearing it once — that's the test of a good one. Then the lyric paints success that compounds ("on and on and on") and names the engine behind it: the six spokes of the Growth Hub. Vision of success is what the hero is really buying.
The Transformation — The Mission
This is our Story: Contribution Plus Service Equals Value. Empower People to Do Good Work. Every day.
Why this works: the close zooms out from one deal to identity — who we are when we do this work. StoryBrand calls it transformation: the customer ends somewhere better, and so do we. "Every day" turns the story into a standard. It's not a campaign; it's a cadence.

The StoryBrand Framework — SB7 Defined

Donald Miller's framework from Building a StoryBrand: seven story elements every clear message contains. Here's each one, and where it lives in the anthem.

1A Character

The customer is the hero — never the brand. They want something specific. In the anthem: "a person carrying a problem they can't solve alone."

2Has a Problem

External (the situation), internal (the frustration), philosophical (why it's just wrong). In the anthem: too many vendors, feeling stuck, nobody accountable.

3Meets a Guide

The brand enters as the experienced guide — empathy plus authority. In the anthem: "They are the hero; we are their Guide."

4Who Gives Them a Plan

A simple path that removes the risk of doing business. In the anthem: discover the problem → bring the plan → do the work in the open.

5And Calls Them to Action

Heroes act when challenged. Direct ("start today") or transitional ("see the proof"). In the room: this is where you ask for the walk, the audit, the meeting.

6That Helps Them Avoid Failure

What's at stake if nothing changes. In the anthem: blown schedules, dark sites, bleeding budgets — the cost of staying stuck.

7And Ends in Success

Paint the win — specific, repeatable, lasting. In the anthem: "success goes on and on and on" — results that feed back into people.

Why It Works

The brain conserves calories. People don't buy the best products — they buy the ones they can understand fastest. Story is the oldest compression format humans have: it organizes information so the brain doesn't have to work. When we pitch in story, the customer stops burning energy decoding us and starts seeing themselves in the win.

The customer is the hero, so the pitch is about them. Most companies position themselves as the hero — "look at our footprint, our awards, our fleet." Heroes don't hire heroes. Heroes hire guides. When FSG takes the Guide's seat, every capability we mention stops being a brag and becomes evidence the hero will make it.

Clarity beats cleverness. If a facility leader can repeat our story to their boss after hearing it once — hero, villain, guide, plan, win — the message travels without us in the room. That's what an elevator pitch is actually for: not to close, but to be repeatable.

One story, every surface. The same 90 seconds works as an anthem at kickoff, an elevator pitch in a lobby, the opening of a proposal, and the hub's homepage. One story, told everywhere, is a brand. Ten stories, told occasionally, is noise.

If you confuse, you lose. If you clarify, you close.
