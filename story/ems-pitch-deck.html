Why

The Problem
Is Silent

A cooler drifts past 41°F overnight. A thermostat walks from 74 to 68. A closed store runs a full occupied day. Nobody sees it until the bill or the spoilage.

Zero visibility is the most expensive line item nobody budgets for
Name the problem before the product
Why · Problem4 min
Ask
  • "If a walk-in failed at 2 a.m. tonight, how would you find out, and when?"
  • "What's your setpoint and schedule standard, and how do you know a site is actually on it?"
  • "How many service calls last year ended with 'it was just a setting'?"
Know
  • Setpoint: the target temperature a unit works to. Chariot shows a heating setpoint (flame) and a cooling setpoint (snowflake); occupied and unoccupied pairs follow the schedule.
  • Drift: a manager nudges the thermostat and nobody notices. Across 500 sites, one degree of drift is a real number. Don't quote it; let the pilot measure it.
  • 41°F is the food-code cold-holding line. Every store ops lead knows it. Use it.
Objection
  • "Our stores call us when something's wrong." → "They call you when they notice. The cooler doesn't call anyone. What's the gap between when it fails and when someone notices?"
Write down their answers. You'll build their rule from them on the alerts slide.

Why · Trigger4 min

How · See5 min
Ask
  • "Who on your team would open this every morning? What would they do differently?"
  • "When a store says it's hot, what happens next today?"
Know
  • Grid view columns: Zone, Space Temp, Fan, Fan Mode, Supply Temp, Occupied setpoints, Unoccupied setpoints, System Mode. Lighting zones below.
  • Is it working? When a unit is actively cooling, supply air is usually 16–20°F below the space temperature. A much smaller difference while running means a problem worth reporting. This is the single most useful thing a rep can teach a facilities lead.
  • Trend resolution: Low ≈ every 2 h (months, fast) · Medium ≈ 15 min (most reviews) · High ≈ 5 min · Show all data for one bad day.
  • Deep Control: everything about one unit — adjustable settings on top (Unoccupied, Occupied, Deadband, Fan Mode, System Mode), read-only status below.
Objection
  • "Our people aren't technical." → "The user guide is written for store managers. No technical background needed, and each person gets their own login with only what they should touch."

How · Control5 min
Ask
  • "Of HVAC, lighting and refrigeration, which one generates the most calls? Which one costs the most when it fails?"
  • "Who owns refrigeration — facilities or operations? Who gets the call when product is lost?"
  • "What else is in the store that stops sales when it fails?" (fuel dispensers, car wash, fountain, kitchen)
Know
  • Deadband: the minimum gap between heating and cooling setpoints so a unit doesn't flip back and forth. Energy lives in the deadband.
  • Astronomical scheduling: lights follow sunrise/sunset by location, not a fixed clock. Daylight harvesting dims when natural light is enough.
  • KE2 Therm: refrigeration controller on cooler/freezer evaporators for on-demand defrost, integrated to Chariot with alarms. Demand-based defrost only runs when ice is actually there.
  • Digital food safety: cold- and hot-holding exception monitoring with probe-problem and logging-gap detection and an audit-ready temperature history.
  • Refrigeration is highlighted because it's where the alarms and the savings are. It's also what moves a site from Core to Standard.
Objection
  • "We only need HVAC." → "Then start at Core. Standard is there when refrigeration is ready. Same platform; nothing to rip out."
