The 30-Second Explain

What this agreement actually is

One Statement of Work under one Master Service Agreement. It locks in four things for every one of the client's locations: what we do (scope), how fast we show up (SLA), what it costs (one national rate card), and the rules that protect both sides (terms). Sign once, covered everywhere.

Say it like this

"This is one agreement that covers every site you have — lighting, electrical, technology, signage. One number to call, one rate card, one team accountable. You stop managing vendors and start managing one partner."

  • NTE limits: nothing past $1,000 standard / $2,000 emergency without written approval

Objection Handling

When they push back

"My local guy is $75 an hour."
Move the conversation from rate per hour to cost per resolved work order. The cheap hour comes with untracked response, no SLA, rework risk, thin insurance, and the client's own time spent chasing it — at one site. Across a portfolio, that overhead multiplies by every vendor.
"What does your local rate cost you after the second visit to fix the same thing — and who's managing fifty of those relationships? Our rate includes the showing up, the doing it right once, and the proving it."
"What's this $150 service fee?"
It's one fee per work order — not per visit, not per call. It funds the 24/7 machine: live intake, dispatch, coordination, and expediting a licensed tech to the door. Framed right, it's the cheapest employee they never had to hire.
"That's your 24/7 service desk, dispatcher, and coordinator — one flat fee per work order, no admin charges hiding anywhere else on the invoice."
"Why are some states more expensive?"
Licensed electrical labor genuinely costs more in those markets — and we publish the list up front instead of surprising them invoice by invoice. Two columns, no asterisk games.
"Same transparency both columns — we'd rather show you the real market up front than win the signature and lose the trust."
"How do I know costs won't run away?"
This is where the agreement sells itself: NTE limits ($1,000 standard / $2,000 emergency — written approval beyond), open-book materials at Cost + 20%, and rate increases capped and indexed to federal labor data with 90 days' notice.
"Nothing material happens without your written yes. The guardrails are in the contract, not in a promise."
"Safety is your problem, not mine."
Flip it — safety is their liability the moment anyone touches energized equipment on their property. An OSHA-compliant, fully insured crew with lockout/tagout discipline is risk leaving their balance sheet.
"Every hour our crew is on your site under our EMR and our umbrella policy is an hour of risk that isn't yours. That's in the rate too."

Don't do this

  • Don't apologize for the rate or open with a discount — the rate is the product working as designed.
  • Don't compare hourly to hourly. Always total cost per resolved work order, portfolio-wide.
  • Don't skip the guardrails (NTE, open book, capped increases) — they close more deals than the rates do.
Section 4 · Terms in Plain English

The rules that protect both sides

Term & Exit

One year, auto-renews. Either side can walk with 60 days' notice. Nobody's trapped — the work earns the renewal.

Payment

Net 30. Rate changes get 90 days' written notice. Disputed charges: flag within 10 business days with documentation.

Warranty

Professional, workmanlike, code-compliant — and materials warranted one year from install. Manufacturer warranties pass through to the client.

Insurance

Workers' comp at statutory limits, $1M general liability per occurrence, $5M umbrella. Certificates on request.
