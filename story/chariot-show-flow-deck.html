Scene 3a — the thermostat card
How · See3 min
Say
  • "Big number is the space temp. Flame is heat, snowflake is cool. COOLING 1 means first stage of cooling; STANDBY means it's satisfied. The little line is the last few hours."

  • "Who would open this every morning? What would they do differently?"

Scene 4 — control, and who gets it
How · Control4 min
Say
  • "That change went to the rooftop unit just now. Your facilities lead can do that from a phone. Your store manager can't, because you decide who can change what."
Ask
  • "Who should be allowed to change a setpoint, and who actually does today?"
Know
  • Deadband: the minimum gap between heating and cooling setpoints so the unit doesn't flip back and forth. Energy lives in the deadband. Don't quote a savings number; let the pilot measure it.
  • Fan Mode: Auto runs the fan only while heating or cooling; Continuous keeps it running for ventilation. System Mode: Auto / Off and others depending on the thermostat.
  • Guide's own caution: your organization may have standard setpoints — check with the facilities lead before changing; changes take effect immediately.
Objection
  • "Managers will override it." → "Lockouts and permissions. They can see; they can't undo the standard."

Closed Means Closed

Schedules decide when a site is occupied, and therefore which setpoints and lighting states apply. Exceptions override the daily schedule on holidays, early closures and events.

Scene 6 — this is how an argument ends
How · Prove5 min
Say
  • "A store says it's hot. A tech says the unit's running. Chariot shows the unit's running and the supply air is warm, which means it's not cooling. You dispatch with a diagnosis, not a complaint."
Ask
  • "When a store complains it's hot, what happens next today? How many of those truck rolls find nothing?"
Know
  • Resolution: Low ≈ one reading every 2 h (long ranges, fast) · Medium ≈ 15 min (most reviews) · High ≈ 5 min (short ranges) · Show all data (one bad day). If a chart won't load, shorten the range or lower the resolution.
  • Calculation Groups combine points into a calculated series. Camera icon saves a picture of the chart for the work order.

How · 10 · Rules

The Text at 2:15 a.m.

A rule watches points and sends a notification when something is wrong. Conditions by Site, Site Label, Equipment Label or Point Label. A time condition filters out spikes. Priority Critical / High / Normal / Low. Phone or email, as many recipients as you need.

"Walk-in cooler above 41°F for 15 minutes"
The rule every store ops lead writes first.
More than / less than, equal, active, in or out of range
Against a value or against another point — space temp above its own cooling setpoint.
Shared rules, on/off switch, custom message
{{site_name}} in the text, so the alert says where.
Scene 7 — build their rule, out loud
How · Act5 min
Say
  • "You told me earlier you'd find out about that cooler from the morning shift. This is the rule that texts you at 2:15 instead. Fifteen minutes so a door open during restocking doesn't page anyone."
Ask
  • "What are the three things you'd want a text about before anyone on site knows?"
Know
  • Condition types from the guide: is more than / is less than; is more than (point) / is less than (point); is equal to / is equal to (point); is Active / is Inactive; is outside range / is within range.
  • Set Points and Send To FlightDeck at the bottom of a rule perform automatic actions when it triggers — ask FSG Support before using. Say "the rule can act, not just alert."
  • Too many alerts? Increase the time condition, narrow the value, or limit to a timeframe.
Objection
  • "We'll drown in alerts." → "That's what the time condition, priorities and FSG's alarm triage are for. You see what matters; we clear the noise."
