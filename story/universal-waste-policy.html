Handling & Disposal

Lamps, ballasts, batteries, and mercury devices. On every retrofit, repair, removal, and installation, FSG manages the waste we generate under the EPA Universal Waste Rule (40 CFR Part 273) and the applicable state program — from removal through recycling — so your client's team never touches it.

Why It's in the Pitch

Compliance is part of what they're buying

Your facilities stay compliant. Your records stay complete. Every retrofit generates waste the EPA regulates — fluorescent lamps, ballasts, batteries, mercury devices. Handled wrong, that's the client's liability. Handled by FSG, it's already in the scope: removed, containerized, recycled through permitted facilities, documented.

Say it like this

"Every lamp and ballast that comes off your ceiling is regulated waste the moment we remove it. Our crews handle it under the EPA Universal Waste Rule — nothing goes in a dumpster, nothing gets left on your dock, and the recycling is documented. That's built into the program, not an add-on."

The Process

How we handle it, every site

1

Identify

Fluorescent, CFL, HID and UV lamps, PCB and non-PCB ballasts, batteries, and mercury devices are classified as universal waste before removal begins.

2

Remove intact

Lamps are removed whole. Crushing, breaking, or drum-top crushing on client property is not permitted.

3

Containerize

Waste goes into closed, structurally sound containers labeled "Universal Waste – Lamps" (or the matching stream) with the accumulation start date.

4

Remove from your facility

FSG crews take the materials off site at the close of work unless the client directs us to use their own on-site recycling containers. Nothing is left behind in a mechanical room or dock.

5

Bulk-recycle at FSG facilities

Materials are consolidated at FSG locations and recycled in bulk through permitted universal-waste recyclers. Bulk volume earns the lowest recycling cost, and that saving is passed to you. Nothing goes in dumpsters or general trash.

6

Consolidate across the program

On national rollouts and service work, small retrofits and repairs are combined into program-level batches. That eliminates the labor and time of recycling site by site and keeps your cost down.

The Exception Plan

If a lamp breaks

Contain

Clear and ventilate the area; keep people out for 15 minutes.

Collect

No vacuuming or sweeping of fragments. Debris is collected with stiff card and tape into a sealed, labeled container and managed as universal waste.

Report

The incident is noted in the project record and reported to the client's site contact.

State rules can be stricter

Where a state program (for example California, Minnesota or Vermont) exceeds federal requirements, FSG follows the state rule. Our crews are trained on this policy before mobilizing.
