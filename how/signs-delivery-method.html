How We Deliver a Sign Program

Nine stages, tracked site by site in the FSG Signs app  ·  nothing is priced before the client approves the drawing, nothing is built before they approve the price

Every site in a program runs as its own project in the FSG Signs app and moves through the same stages. Walk a buyer through this page and they see exactly what they get, when they sign off, and how one partner carries it from site list to service. The examples are real FSG deliverables from Wesco (an Anixter-to-Wesco rebrand) and H-E-B (a new store).

1Site Provided2Site Survey3Brandbook Dev.4Brandbook Approval5Estimate6Estimate Approval7Permitting8Production9Support
1
Start

Site Provided

The client sends the site list. Each site is set up as its own project with an address, site contact, priority and work order.

Client gets: one project record per site and a single FSG project manager.
2
Field

Site Survey

A crew visits every site, with a bucket truck where needed. Every existing sign is photographed and measured; mounting surface, structure and power are checked; any extra old branding is flagged.

Client gets: a photo report, measurements and an existing-sign inventory per site. Anything outside scope comes back as a priced change order. Example: Wesco #1029U, North Charleston, SC.
3
Design

Brandbook Development

Designers build the site's sign package from the client's brand standards and the survey: a location plan showing where every sign goes, and a shop drawing for each sign with dimensions, materials, colors, scope and the existing-vs-proposed view.

Client gets: a location plan and one drawing sheet per sign, each tied to its sign letter.
4
Client sign-off

Brandbook Approval

The drawings go to the client for approval. Every revision is versioned (R1, R2, R3…) and the stage stays open until the client signs the approval block on the sheet.

Client gets: control. Nothing is priced or built until they approve what it looks like.
5
Price

Estimate Creation

The site is priced from the approved drawings — in the client's cost template when they have one, otherwise on our five cost lines: surveys, fabrication, removal and installation, packaging and freight, and permits.

Client gets: a per-site price tied to an approved drawing, so the number can't drift from the design.
6
Client sign-off

Estimate Approval

The client approves each site's price. Anything found outside scope is priced as a change order and approved before the work.

Client gets: no surprise invoices.
7
Compliance

Permitting

Sign area is checked against what code allows on every wall, then permits are pulled and filed. Landlord, mall and airport-authority sign rules are tracked per site.

Client gets: a permit-ready set and permit status by site. Example: H-E-B #820, San Antonio.
8
Build & install

Production & Beyond

Seven production steps run site by site: fabrication in our San Antonio shop, packaging and freight, removal and disposal of the old sign, installation, and closeout. Lit signs are engineered down to the LED layout and power, and the general contractor's requirements are called out on the drawing.

Client gets: the sign built, shipped, installed and the old one gone — with one crew lead to call.
9
After launch

Product Support

Warranty and service for the life of the sign. The site record keeps its drawings, photos and service history.

Client gets: a 10-year lifetime warranty on LEDs and a 5-year sign parts and labor warranty — and a service partner who already knows the site.
How to use this with a buyer. Share your screen and scroll it, or walk the nine stages on a whiteboard. Stop at stages 4 and 6 and say it plainly: "You approve the look before we price it, and you approve the price before we build it." Client drawings stay inside the hub — confirm permission before any Wesco or H-E-B sheet goes into a proposal.
