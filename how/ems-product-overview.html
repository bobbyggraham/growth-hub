Why This Product Leads

Energy Performance is FSG's flagship line: "We cut your energy costs." EMS is the product that makes that promise measurable. Every multi-site customer runs HVAC, lighting and refrigeration they cannot see from headquarters, and every one of them is paying for it: drifting setpoints, stores running occupied schedules while closed, coolers failing quietly overnight, truck rolls for problems a phone call could have fixed. EMS puts those systems on one screen, under one standard, with a support center behind it.

It also leads because of where it takes the account. Circle K started with FSG on one EMS challenge and grew into six categories and $300M+ delivered. EMS is the first door into lighting, electrical, signs and the Energy Performance retrofit work that follows, and it comes with a recurring support agreement that keeps FSG in the building every month.

The one-line pitch

FSG Energy Management puts every store's HVAC, lighting and refrigeration on one screen, enforces your standard, and backs it with a 24/7 support center that fixes problems before a truck rolls.

Software is Chariot, FSG's own platform. Hardware is Clarity, built in FSG's UL 508A shop. Crews are FSG's. Support is FSG's. Most EMS vendors sell the screen; FSG sells the outcome and services what it installs, coast-to-coast.

What EMS Is (and Isn't)

An Energy Management System is the controls, sensors, software and people that watch and run a building's energy-using equipment so it does what the business wants, when the business wants, and nothing more. In a c-store or retail site that means the rooftop units, the lighting circuits, the walk-in coolers and freezers, and increasingly the fuel dispensers, car wash, kitchen equipment and EV chargers.

TermWhat it means when you say it to a customer
EMSEnergy Management System. The whole product: controls + software + support. Use this with executives and finance.
BAS / BMSBuilding Automation (or Management) System. The controls layer in the building. Same thing engineers call the EMS; use it with facilities and IT.
ChariotFSG's own web application for monitoring and controlling connected systems across every site. The customer logs in at their own address on fsgchariot.app. Nothing to install.
ClarityFSG's controller family. Clarity Pro Connect is the wired, hardened, UL-listed Niagara 4 panel; Pro Reach is wireless (Zigbee); Agile Connect is the MicroBox package for small retail and car wash.
SetpointThe target temperature a unit works to. Chariot shows a heating setpoint (flame) and a cooling setpoint (snowflake). Occupied and unoccupied pairs follow the schedule.
DeadbandThe gap kept between heating and cooling setpoints so a unit doesn't flip back and forth. Energy lives in the deadband.
DriftWhat happens when a store manager nudges a thermostat and nobody notices. Drift protection locks the standard and reports exceptions.
PointOne value from a device: SpaceTemp, SupplyAirTemp, OccCoolSetPoint, FanStatus. Trends and rules are built on points.
Rule / notificationA condition the customer defines (cooler above 41°F for 15 minutes) that sends a phone or email alert. Every alert is logged.
Demand chargeThe part of a utility bill set by the highest 15-minute peak, not total use. Demand-charge management staggers equipment so the peak never sets.
M&VMeasurement and verification. How a savings claim is proven: baseline, interval data, before and after. Say "measurable" or "verified" savings. Never "guaranteed."
Open protocolBACnet, Modbus, MQTT, API. Means FSG can integrate the equipment already in the building, including other vendors' controls.

What EMS is not: it is not a utility bill audit, not a lighting retrofit, and not an ESCO contract. Those are separate FSG offers; EMS is often how the customer finds out they need them.

Triggers that put EMS on the table

  • A new store prototype, rebrand or remodel wave (controls get specified once and travel).
  • A refrigeration or food-safety incident, or a new foodservice program.
  • A utility rate change, demand-charge spike or ESG reporting requirement.
  • An aging or orphaned controls system the incumbent no longer supports (FSG supports other vendors' equipment).
  • A maintenance budget cut: the customer needs fewer truck rolls, not more technicians.

The pricing rule

  • These are budgetary ranges, as stated in FSG Smart Buildings program pricing. You may quote them as ranges, labeled budgetary, to frame a conversation.
  • A firm per-site price follows a site survey by Smart Buildings sales engineering. Never commit to a number inside the range.
  • Rebates are filed by FSG. Say "we identify and file the rebates." Never estimate a rebate amount to a customer; processing takes 6–8 weeks and estimates are never guaranteed.
  • OpEx / SaaS structures exist. Route them to Watt-checked pricing before they go in writing.

The Questions That Open the Deal

EMS sells on discovery. Ask, then listen. The customer will tell you which tier they are.

Visibility. "If a walk-in cooler failed at 2 a.m. tonight, how would you find out, and when?"
Standard. "What's your store standard for setpoints and schedules, and how do you know a store is on it?"
Cost of a truck roll. "How many service calls last year ended with 'it was just a setting'? What does one of those cost you?"
Incumbent. "What's controlling the HVAC today, who supports it, and what happens when it goes offline?"
Capital program. "What's rolling out across the portfolio in the next 18 months — remodels, foodservice, EV, solar? Who specs the controls?"
Money. "Where does energy show up on your P&L, and who owns that line?"
The small yes. "If we surveyed five of your stores at no cost, which five would teach us the most?"

Common Misses — Check Before You Walk Away

  • Selling the screen. The customer can buy a dashboard anywhere. Sell the standard, the support center and the crews that service what we install.
  • Quoting a number inside the range. Ranges are budgetary. Firm pricing follows the survey. Every time.
  • "Guaranteed savings." FSG is not an ESCO. Say measurable or verified savings, proven with M&V.
  • Skipping the incumbent question. If another vendor's controls are in the building, the opener is multi-vendor support, not displacement.
  • Forgetting the recurring row. Every EMS opportunity has a Complete Support companion. Create both records.
  • Wrong coding. Services · Energy Performance is the default. Distribution only when FSG ships hardware and someone else installs.
  • Named proof in the wrong room. Circle K figures are internal and authorized only. Customer-agnostic proof works everywhere.
