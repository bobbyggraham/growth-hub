The Conversation That Never Sells

The best customer conversation you will have this year won't include a pitch. You won't walk the line card. You may not say "FSG" more than once. And they will still ask when you can come back.

That isn't luck. It's four moves, in order:

Connect

They feel like a person, not a prospect. It starts in your first ten words.

Discover the pain

You find the problem they would pay to fix. They do most of the talking.

Make an impact

They leave with something useful: a clearer picture, a number, a next step.

Earn the next one

A reason to come back, and a project to work on together.

The Standard

If you never pitched and they still want the next meeting, you did it right.

It's happening right now: Sky Chefs

We bid Sky Chefs twice, in 2025 and 2026. One site each, through facilities, nobody warmed up. We lost both. Here is the third try, same customer, different approach.

The moveWhat we did
ConnectA partner, Foodbuy, made the introduction. Before anyone pitched, we built the account plan and ran a short discovery call that was all questions.
Discover the painFace to face, we asked about their rebranding strategy. We learned they run 36 locations, every building is different and decades old, and they are consolidating vendors.
Make an impactThey named six priorities themselves: signage, LED, generators, EV chargers, cabling. An NDA got signed and an MSA approved, so working together is easy.
Earn the next oneTheir procurement director reopened a closed signage RFP so FSG could respond. The proposal covers 37 sites, and four more projects are on the board.
Nobody sold. The quote came last. Watch the whole account move through Engage in Episode 13: Air Support.

This guide breaks that skill into three things you can practice: be dynamic, be magnetic, be authentic.

01Dynamic: Use Your Whole Voice

The idea: your topic keeps someone in the conversation. Your voice decides whether they want the next one. A great point in a flat voice sounds boring, and most of us are flatter than we think.

You have four voices. Most people use one.

Tap a voice to see how it should sound, then say the line out loud.

Match the voice to the moment. Coaching someone who needed a friend costs you trust. Most sellers live in Educator. The connection happens in Friend, and the ask lands in Coach.

Three dials to turn

  • A dimmer, not a switch. Don't just get louder. Bring your volume up on the part that matters and down on the part that's personal.
  • Your first five words carry the energy. "Hey! So good to see you" beats a flat "hey, how's it going." You don't need to be bubbly. You need to sound glad you're there.
  • Own the pause. Pause after the key number and after the ask. Silence reads as confidence.
Practice · 5 minutes

Record a one-minute story from your week on your phone. Record it again as if you're telling a friend you're excited to see. Play both. Then review the better one three ways: sound off to watch your body, sound only to hear your tone, and once more counting filler words.

02Magnetic: Make It About Them

The idea: people are pulled toward whoever makes them feel interesting and understood. The most magnetic person in the room is rarely the one talking the most.

Find the spark

The usual question gets the usual answer. Ask one they haven't heard a hundred times, then watch for the spark: their voice speeds up and the answers get longer. Stay there.

Instead ofTry
How are you?What's the best thing that's happened at your sites this week?
What do you do?What's the most interesting part of your job?
How's the project going?What's the weirdest thing that's come up on this project?
Any needs I can help with?What's the one facilities issue that keeps landing on your desk?

Three follow-ups before you talk about yourself

When they tell you something that matters, ask at least three questions about it before you add anything of your own. "Tell me more about that" counts. The second and third answers are where the real problem lives. Aim for them talking 70% of the time.

Sell the painkiller, never the vitamin

A vitamin is nice to have someday. A painkiller fixes what hurts today. You can't know which one you're holding until they've told you where it hurts, so discover before you describe.

The Peak-End Effect

People don't remember a conversation minute by minute. They remember two things: the peak, the single strongest moment, and the end. Everything else fades. So design both on purpose.

Build it on purposeWhat kills it
The peakThe moment they feel understood. Play it back: "So what I'm hearing is ___. Did I get that right?"A twenty-minute capability walk with no high point
The endOne clear next step with a date, said warmly, by name"Let me know if you have questions," or ending on Q&A
Practice · every conversation this week

Before you walk in, write down two lines: the moment you want them to remember, and the last sentence you'll say. After, write down whether both happened.

03Authentic: Say Less, Mean It

The idea: talking more does not show more value. It shows nerves. Being authentic means being clear, being brief, and being the same person when nothing is on the line.

Tell them the time. Don't build the clock.

The customer asked what time it is. They did not ask how the watch works. Lead with the end state, the result they get. Leave the process in your pocket until they ask for it.

Building the clockTelling the time
"First our team does a photometric study, then we spec fixtures, then procurement…""Your lots are lit, your energy bill drops, and your team stops calling about outages."
"Let me walk you through how our service model is structured.""One call, one partner, every site."
Twelve slides on who we areOne sentence on what changes for them

3 · 2 · 1: how to never ramble

Put on the spot? Don't wing it. Pick one of these three shapes and your answer has a beginning and an end.

3

Steps

"There are three steps." Use it for how something happens.

"Three steps: we walk the site, we price it, we schedule around your hours."

2

Ways

"There are two ways to do this." Use it when they have a choice.

"Two ways: all sites this year, or the worst ten first."

1

Thing

"If you remember one thing." Use it for the point.

"The one thing: every dark lot is costing you after sundown."

Then stop. Say the three, the two, or the one, and let the silence work. A fourth point erases the first three.

Be the same person off the clock

  • Do what you said you'd do. Especially when you don't feel like it. That's how people learn to trust you, and how you learn to trust yourself.
  • How you made them feel is the brand. They'll forget what you said and what you sold. They won't forget how they felt.
  • Keep a story bank. Three or four short, true stories from your week in a note on your phone. Real ones beat rehearsed ones.
  • New isn't fake. A voice you haven't used yet will feel awkward. That's a skill you haven't practiced, not a person you aren't.
Practice · 2 minutes, with a partner

Have someone throw you a question about anything: your weekend, a product, a customer. Answer in 3-2-1. One thing, two ways, three steps. Switch. Do it five times.

The Practice Plan: One Habit a Week

Reading this changes nothing. Reps do. One habit a week, every conversation, at work and at home.

  1. Week 1 · Dynamic. Record yourself twice a week. Use all four voices on purpose in one meeting. Fix your first five words.
  2. Week 2 · Magnetic. One spark question and three follow-ups in every conversation. Write down the peak and the end before each meeting.
  3. Week 3 · Authentic. Answer every on-the-spot question in 3-2-1. Catch yourself building the clock and switch to the time.
  4. Week 4 · Put it together. Run one full customer conversation without pitching. Score it below.
