02How To Use It — Three Moments

The rule: the hub is worth nothing on a laptop at your desk. It's worth everything ten minutes before a meeting, in the room, and the hour after you win.

Before — the 10-minute prep Vision · Execution · Finance · Results
  1. Name the hero and the problem. One sentence, in their words, before you open anything.
  2. Pull the vertical playbook. Solutions chip → their vertical. Steal the opening question and the two credibility markers.
  3. Load one proof. Results chip → filter to their vertical or their cell. Same-cell proof first — a service buyer doesn't care about your construction milestones.
  4. Know the money. The Products button in the search bar → Products × Delivery Method for how they'll buy it; ROI Calculator under Development if savings will come up.

That's it. Story, plan, proof, price — ten minutes, four clicks.

During — in the room People · Vision · Finance
  • Lead with them, not us. Their problem out loud first. The Master Presentation is backup, not the opening act.
  • Show one proof, not five. Open the card, let the numbers and the quote do the work, close it. Five proofs reads as a brochure; one reads as evidence.
  • Draw the pinwheel. Whiteboard, napkin, back of the scope — six spokes and value at the center explains who we are in under two minutes.
  • Pull the hub up live. It's gated and it's ours — showing a client the matrix or a proof card on your phone lands harder than a PDF you'll email later.
After — close the loop Execution · Results
  • Log it in Engage the same day. Customer Problem and Solution Description in the client's own words — those two fields are what the proof engine and every future deck are built from. Stuck on a field? Development chip → FSG App FAQs, and type the question.
  • Report before anyone asks. Measure it, track it, report it. That habit is the whole Execution spoke.
  • When it lands, build the proof. Don't wait for someone to ask you for a case study. Section 04.

04Get My Proof Live

Why this play matters: every rep asks "do we have a story like this customer?" The answer is always yes — it's just buried in Engage notes, invoices, and somebody's memory. Proof Builder is how your win stops being tribal knowledge and becomes ammunition the whole org can fire. Every card carries ★ Proof submitted by [your name], and the contributor board keeps score.

1. Tell the story
2. Drop the files
3. Check permissions
4. Generate & send
5. QA → live on the hub
1
Tell The Story

Development chip → Proof Builder. Fill it in order — it takes longer to find the files than to write the words.

  • Submitted by / your name / your email — this is the credit line. Don't skip it.
  • Client name — legal-ish and consistent ("Ulta Beauty", not "Ulta").
  • Vertical — one of thirteen, from Commercial Real Estate & Office through Multifamily. Pick how the customer would describe themselves.
  • The cell — Product line (Electrical · Lighting · Signs · Technology Infrastructure · Energy Performance) × Delivery method (Construction · Service · Distribution) × Type (LED retrofit, emergency, rebrand rollout, mission critical, EMS monitoring…). The cell is how the engine matches your proof to the next prospect, so get it right — a lighting retrofit tagged "Electrical" surfaces for the wrong buyer.
  • The problem — their words, their pain, before we showed up. Two or three sentences. Don't name FSG here.
  • The solution — what we did, scope and scale. Outcome first, then how.
  • Client quote & attribution — one real sentence from a real person with their real title. Spell the name right; we verify it.
Multi-scope job? One card per cell. A rebrand rollout with sign fabrication and electrical service is two proofs with their own dollars — and two roster spots instead of one.
2
Load The Numbers

Numbers are what separate a proof card from a testimonial.

  • ★ Gold stat — dollars first. The headline number, and it should be money if money is available: contract value, annual savings, cost avoided.
  • Every stat needs a number and a label. "328 locations", "18 weeks", "42% energy reduction". A stat slot is not a place for a program name — that goes in the Program field.
  • Only claims the record supports. Pull from the order, the invoice, or approved site copy. Nothing estimated, nothing rounded up. If it isn't in the record, leave it out.
3
Drop The Files
  • Client logos — up to 4. Transparent PNG or SVG if you have it.
  • Job photos — up to 6. This is where most submissions lose their shine, so: shoot landscape, get the work in frame (fixture, sign, panel, gear — not an empty parking lot), and check the orientation after you upload. Phone photos routinely arrive rotated because the upload strips the EXIF data. Keep them tight and reasonably sized; the gold frame comes from the card itself.
  • Proposal / backup link — the Drive or Engage link that proves the numbers. Nobody sees it but QA; it's what makes the card auditable.
4
Check Permissions

Four toggles, and they're the ones that keep us out of trouble: name approved · logo approved · numbers approved · quote approved.

  • You attest to them as the submitter — so know the answer, don't guess it. Account-owner verification is where we're headed.
  • Anything not approved renders anonymized ("a national beauty retailer, 1,400 stores"). Still useful, still proof.
  • Scrub as you write. No portal URLs, logins, passwords, or personal contact info in any free-text field. Engage records are full of them; proof cards never are.
5
Generate, Send & Go Live

Four buttons at the bottom. Use the first and the last, every time:

  • Download Proof Card — your finished HTML card. Yours to show a client today, before it's even published.
  • Copy Deck Snippet — drop the proof straight into a deck or one-pager.
  • Copy Proof Record — the paste-ready record for the engine (QA uses this; grab it if you're asked).
  • Email Growth Hub — the submit button. It downloads the card (logos and photos baked in) and opens the email to Bobby with you copied and the record in the body. Attach the downloaded card before you hit send — the browser can't attach it for you, and that one step is the difference between a submission and a to-do.

Then what happens: QA pass on the hub side — scrub, copy into FSG voice, stat repair, photo orientation and crop, quote and cell verification — then the record goes into the Proof Engine. One entry, three places: your card appears in the Results grid (searchable and filterable, click for the full pop-out), the client's chip on Products × Delivery Method becomes clickable and pops the same card, and the proof enters the library the deck generator pulls from. Turnaround is days, not weeks. If something's missing, you'll hear which field.

What Makes A Card Land

Do this

  • Money in the gold stat
  • The customer's problem in the customer's words
  • One named human in the quote, with a title
  • A photo of the actual work, right way up
  • The cell tagged the way the buyer buys
  • Submitted the week it closed

Not this

  • "Significant savings" with no number
  • A program name sitting in a stat slot
  • An unattributed quote, or a paraphrase
  • A stock photo, or an empty storefront
  • Numbers nobody approved
  • Anything copied out of Engage without a scrub

The three card statuses

Live  Full record — problem, solution, numbers, quote, logo, photo, permissions confirmed.

In build  A placeholder holding the roster spot while logos, photos, numbers, or the quote are still pending. If you see a client you work with sitting In Build, that card is asking you for a submission — and it's the fastest name you'll ever get on the board.

Confidential  Renders without numbers, internal use only. Some clients are worth having quietly.

Your 15-Minute Version

  • Gather first: the order or invoice, one quote from a real person, two job photos, the client logo.
  • Open Proof Builder. Name, email, client, vertical.
  • Tag the cell — product line × delivery method × type.
  • Problem in their words. Solution, outcome first.
  • Gold stat in dollars. Every other stat gets a number and a label.
  • Upload logo and photos. Check the photos aren't rotated.
  • Four permission toggles. Scrub the free text.
  • Email Growth Hub → attach the card it just downloaded → send.
  • Use your card in the next meeting. Don't wait for it to publish.
