Brand standards — the engine, the story, the voice, and the visual system. Growth Hub is the growth engine of FSG for business, personal, and community.

The story

Every engagement starts with a person carrying a problem they can't solve alone — a client, a teammate, a neighbor. They are the hero. Growth Hub is the guide.

We don't chase transactions; we build an institution dedicated to timeless contribution and service. The guide shows up with a destination worth trusting, a plan that develops the whole person — business, personal, faith, community — and then does the work: measured, tracked, reported. Cash keeps the engine honest. The hero gets the win they wanted, and it goes on the board.

Then the flywheel turns: every solved problem creates the next hero who trusts the guide. Protect the relationships we've earned. Land the next one. Expand what's possible together.

The equation

Contribution + Service = Value
Value sits at the center of everything. Meaning is the echo.

The six spokes

Six lanes, one loop, value at the core. Read clockwise from the top — People always walk in first.

People The hero with a problem walks in first. Everything comes from people — the client, the teammate, the community.

Vision We show up as the guide with a destination worth trusting. Carried by the story, the tagline, and Y+1.

Development The plan: business, personal, faith, community. Development is the key — it's what makes people valuable. (Client-facing shorthand: business, personal, and community impact.)

Execution The guide acts: measure, track, report. Systems, ERP, equipment, and partnerships doing the work in the open.

Finance Cash keeps the engine honest and funded — growth, stress, daily KPIs.

Results The hero gets the win they wanted, and it goes on the board. Top-line revenue, bottom-line cost, lives measurably better.

The flywheel: Results feed straight back into People — every solved problem creates the next hero who trusts the guide.

The voice

Growth Hub sounds like a trusted guide: confident, plain-spoken, generous. Never the hero of its own story.

  • The hero leads the sentence. Write "you" before "we." The reader's win is the headline; our role is the plan behind it.
  • Scoreboard honesty. Numbers over adjectives. If it happened, show the result; if it didn't, say what's next.
  • Short and certain. Punchy verbs, short sentences, no hype, no filler. Say it the way you'd say it across a job-site table.
  • Contribution first. Every message answers one question: what did this add — to the business, the person, or the community?
  • Warm, not soft. Encourage hard, hold accountable harder. The guide believes in the hero enough to tell the truth.

The style

  • One gold rule. Gold marks value and nothing else — the core, the win, the key lane. One gold element per layout; when everything glitters, nothing does.
  • Navy is the field, white is the air. Deep navy carries authority; generous white space carries clarity. Steel and ice do the supporting work.
  • Rounded, human type. The wordmark and headlines use rounded letterforms — approachable strength, matched to the FSG logotype. No gold underlines, ever.
  • The spoke carries the story. The six-spoke hub is the primary mark — value at the gold center, always, in the lockup and standing alone. The FSG star is a reserved asset: co-branded moments and win markers only, never in the primary lockup.
  • Clear space and minimums. Keep clear space equal to the star's width on all sides of the logo. Don't set the full logo below 120px wide; use the spoke mark or favicon below that.
  • Never do. Don't stretch, rotate, recolor, outline, or drop-shadow the marks. Don't set the wordmark in a square-cornered font. Don't put navy type on navy.
