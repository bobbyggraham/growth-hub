The Engage Mark

Three blue bars, one gold arrow, and an open side. The logo is the job description — who we are on an account, and why we were chosen, called, and sent.
Guide  ·  October 2026  ·  Engage  ·  Internal Enablement

Why a Logo Needs a Meaning

Speak So They Buy says it plainly: put your ideas into shapes, because the brain keeps a picture long after it drops a paragraph. The Engage mark is our shape. If a rep can draw it on a napkin and say what each piece means, they can explain the whole growth organization to a new hire, a customer, or their own kid in under a minute.

Scroll, and the mark builds itself one piece at a time. Every piece is a role or a calling. By the end you'll have the whole thing — and you'll never look at the login screen the same way.

The Core Principle

Three roles, one arrow. The roles hold the account. The arrow is why we're on it. Contribution + Service = Value.

The mark starts empty. Scroll to build it.

01The Upright — The Guide

Level 1 · Human engagement, buddy to buddy

The piece: the tall blue bar. It goes in first because nothing else stands without it. Every other piece attaches to the upright.

The role: the Guide is the first voice the customer knows. No pitch. Shows up, listens, helps, keeps showing up. Runs the 5-Star Lead test on every relationship — Willing to Engage, Friendly and Collaborative, Knows What They Want, Knows When They Want It, Knows They Want It From YOU — and logs every meeting, even the ones with no deal attached.

John 10:3–4

“He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out… his sheep follow him because they know his voice.”

The Good Shepherd doesn't herd. He calls by name, and they follow because they recognize the voice. That's the Guide's whole job: be the voice they know. Wayne Covington at Reformation answered 46 review notes in one day. He didn't do that for a vendor. He did it for a voice.

Transition trigger: five stars lit on the relationship → the Guide hands to Level 2 with the record complete. The upright stays. It always stays.

02The Top Bar — Business Development

Level 2 · Sower and Closer · Land & Expand

The piece: the top bar, reaching out from the upright toward the open side. It's the reach.

The role: Business Development sows new ground and closes it. Lands the first category, then expands into the next one inside the same account — or goes back into an at-risk account and wins it again. Owns the face-to-face, the account plan that comes out of it, and the internal meeting that builds the team around the account.

John 9:7

“Go, wash in the Pool of Siloam” (this word means “Sent”).

One instruction: go. The pool is named Sent, and the One who sends him was Himself sent. BD is the sent role. You don't wait for the account to come to you. You go to Merrick Park. You go to the design review. You go find the next category.

Transition trigger: account built out, customer says it's healthy → BD hands to Level 3. On Reformation that's Bobby and Donn handing to Tiffany at the QBR, with Wayne in the room.

03The Bottom Bar — Account Manager

Level 3 · Protect · Manage · Enrich

The piece: the bottom bar. The foundation. It carries the weight of everything above it, and it's what the customer stands on for years.

The role: the Account Manager keeps what was won and makes it worth more. Protect: every open action item closed in writing, no stale records, no surprises. Manage: the cadence — daily swings, weekly clear-the-bases, monthly check-your-average. Enrich: the contact map grows, the plan gets re-versioned, service turns into a program.

John 10:28–29

“They shall never perish; no one will snatch them out of my hand… no one can snatch them out of my Father's hand.”

That's Protect in one line. No one snatches the account out of your hand. Not a competitor's local sub, not a rep change, not a login error on day one. The Account Manager is the hand that holds.

Shadows from week one. The AM is in the internal meeting before the handoff, so the second handoff is invisible to the customer.

04The Triangle — One Team

Guide · Business Development · Account Manager

Look at the three bars together and you'll see it: a triangle. Upright, top, bottom — three corners, one account. Speak So They Buy already taught the shape: move one corner, the others move too.

Pull the Guide out and the customer stops recognizing the voice. Pull BD out and nothing new ever lands. Pull the AM out and what you landed slides away. A level is a job on the account, not a rank. One rep can carry all three corners on a small account. On a $1.5M pipeline with 23 open opportunities, it takes a team — and the team has to meet.

Protect · Land · Expand is the triangle in motion. AM protects. BD lands and expands. The Guide is why the door was open in the first place.

05The Arrow — Chosen

The gold begins

The piece: the first length of the gold arrow, starting inside the mark. Gold is the only color that isn't a role. It's the reason for the roles.

John 9:1–3

“Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?” … “Neither… this happened so that the works of God might be displayed in him.”

A man born blind. The disciples want to know whose fault it is. Jesus says it was never about fault — this man was chosen for this moment. In John 10 He says it plainly: His flock doesn't belong to the world. They were given to Him.

For us: you weren't handed an account by accident. You were put on it. The first length of gold is you deciding to act like the person who was picked for this one.

06The Arrow — Called

The gold extends

The piece: the arrow reaches the open side. It's about to leave the mark.

John 9:35 · John 10:3

Jesus heard that they had thrown him out, and when he found him, he said, “Do you believe in the Son of Man?” … “He calls his own sheep by name.”

The religious leaders throw the healed man out of the synagogue. Jesus goes and finds him. Calls him. He follows, because he knows the voice. Called isn't a feeling. It's a name spoken and a person who answers.

For us: you're called by name to this work. So is the customer — by you. Know their name, their role, what they said in their words. That's why the meeting gets logged.

07The Arrow — Sent

The gold leaves the mark

The piece: the arrowhead, outside the bars, pointing out. This is why the mark is open on the right. It isn't a letter missing a side. It's a door left open on purpose, so the arrow can go.

John 9:7 · John 9:25

“Go, wash in the Pool of Siloam” (this word means “Sent”). So the man went and washed, and came home seeing. … “One thing I do know. I was blind but now I see!”

Mud on the eyes and one instruction. He obeys, he sees, and he's immediately in a hostile room being interrogated — and he testifies anyway. “I was blind but now I see.” That's the testimony. That's the whole point of being sent.

For us: you're sent into the account. Do the work in the open. Then bring back the testimony — the proof card, the logged meeting, the win on the board. Sent isn't finished until someone can say what changed.

08The Whole Mark

Contribution + Service = Value

Three bars. One arrow. An open side. Now read the lockup the way it was written:

  • ENGAGE — the verb. Not a system we log into; a thing we do.
  • Contribution + Service = Value — the equation the triangle runs on. Contribute something, serve someone, and value shows up on its own.
  • Chosen · Called · Sent — the arrow, in order.
  • Empowering people to do good work. — the sentence. The customer is the hero; we're the guide with the plan.

Say it in thirty seconds

“Three bars, one arrow. The Guide is the upright — the voice they know. Business Development is the top bar — sent to land and expand. The Account Manager is the bottom bar — the hand that holds. The gold arrow is why we're here: chosen, called, sent. And it points out, because the work is out there.”

Draw It — The Napkin Version

You have a whiteboard, a napkin, or the back of a scope. Ten seconds:

  1. Vertical line. “This is the Guide. Everything hangs on the relationship.”
  2. Top line out to the right. “Business Development. We go land it and expand it.”
  3. Bottom line out to the right. “Account Manager. We hold it — nobody takes it out of our hand.”
  4. Arrow through the middle, out the open side. “And this is why we're on your account at all. We were sent.”
Talk Track — New Rep, First Week “Before you learn a single screen in Engage, learn the mark. Three roles, one arrow. Whatever account you touch, ask which corner you're standing in today — and who's standing in the other two.”
Talk Track — Customer, When They Ask About the Logo “The three bars are three people on your account — the one you know, the one who brings the next thing, and the one who makes sure it never slips. The arrow's the part we take seriously: we're sent to do good work for you, not to sell you something.”

Rollout: Learn It, Draw It, Live It

  1. Week 1 — Learn it. Every rep can name the four pieces and the three verses behind the arrow. Managers ask in the huddle: “Which corner are you on Reformation?”
  2. Week 2 — Draw it. Once on a whiteboard, once on a napkin, once for a customer who asks. The 10-second version.
  3. Week 3 — Live it. Every account plan names all three corners. Every internal meeting invites by corner. Every win comes back as a proof card.

The Payoff

A logo you can explain is a strategy you can run. Three bars, one arrow, an open door. Chosen, called, sent — and empowered to do good work.
