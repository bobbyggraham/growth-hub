Speak So They Buy

The FSG Sales Communication Playbook — three field-ready techniques to make every pitch, proposal walkthrough, and follow-up impossible to forget.
Sales Leadership  ·  August 2026  ·  Internal Enablement

Why This Playbook Exists

Getting a customer to listen is table stakes. Getting them to remember is how deals move. Most FSG pitches lose deals not because the solution is wrong, but because three days later the facility director can't repeat what we said to their CFO.

This playbook gives every FSG seller three techniques to make our message stick — in discovery calls, proposal reviews, and every follow-up in between.

The Core Principle

You're not selling to the person in the room. You're arming the person who has to sell you to their boss next week. When they believe in you and can repeat you, the deal moves. Make both effortless.

01The Rule of Three

The rule: Never give a customer more than three points at a time. One, two, three — anything past three, they remember nothing. Including the first three.

Where FSG sellers apply it

  • Discovery recap: "I heard three priorities today: energy cost, maintenance burden, and uptime. Did I get those right?"
  • Proposal exec summary: Three outcomes at the top. Everything else is supporting detail.
  • Voicemail / video update: One update, one insight, one ask. Done.
  • Objection handling: Answer with three reasons, then stop talking.
Talk Track — LED Retrofit Pitch "There are three ways this retrofit pays for you: your energy bill drops the first month, your maintenance calls nearly disappear, and your rebates offset the install. Let's walk through each."
Field test: If your customer can't repeat your three points back at the end of the meeting, you had more than three — or you didn't label them as three.

02Put Ideas Into Shapes

The rule: When you're presenting (deck, whiteboard, one-pager), put your key ideas inside simple geometric shapes — circles, triangles, squares. The brain takes a snapshot of a shape far more easily than a paragraph. The customer remembers the picture, and the picture carries your points.

FSG shape library

Three Circles

Value pillars: Cost · Reliability · Sustainability. Where they overlap: FSG.

Triangle

Scope · Schedule · Budget. Move one corner, the others move too.

Four Squares

Assess → Design → Install → Maintain. The FSG lifecycle in one glance.

When it works: Shapes need eyes on them — decks, whiteboards, one-pagers, site-walk sketches. On a phone call or in a hallway conversation, skip the shapes and go straight to Technique 03.

03The 4-Mat: Why · What · How · Now

The rule: Structure any explanation in four frames, in this order. It answers the customer's four built-in objections before they say them out loud.

Why

"Why does this matter right now?"

Urgency and stakes. Rate hikes, failing fixtures, compliance deadlines, budget cycles.

What

"What is this, at 10,000 feet?"

The big picture in three pieces (see Technique 01).

How

"How does this affect me?"

Connect each piece to their site, their budget, their team.

Now

"What do I do with this?"

One domino. A single, specific action step — never a list.

Talk Track — Service Agreement Close "Why now: your fixtures are past rated life and every failure is an emergency call. What: a planned maintenance program with three parts — quarterly inspections, priority response, fixed pricing. How: here's what that means for your two DFW sites specifically. Now: you don't have to remember any of this — just get me 30 minutes with your regional director next week, and I'll bring the one-pager."
The "Now" is the whole game. End every customer interaction with exactly one action step. If they remember nothing else, they remember the domino.

Rollout: One Habit Per Week

Don't try all three at once — that breaks Rule of Three math. Build one habit at a time:

  1. Week 1 — Rule of Three. Use it in every conversation: customer calls, internal syncs, even at home. Managers: listen for it on call reviews.
  2. Week 2 — Shapes. Add one shape to every deck and proposal going out. Whiteboard at least once.
  3. Week 3 — 4-Mat. Structure every pitch and follow-up email as Why → What → How → Now.
  4. Week 4 — Stack them. All three, every customer touch. Sales leaders spot-check one proposal and one recorded call per rep.

The Payoff

Customers don't buy from the seller they liked most. They buy from the one they can re-explain to their boss. Be that seller.
